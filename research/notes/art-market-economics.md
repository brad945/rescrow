# Art market economics: splits, discounts, installments and gallery failure

## What we learned

- Global art sales rose about 4% to an estimated $59.6bn in 2025, after two years of decline; dealer sales were about $34.8bn and public auctions about $20.7bn. Dealers are still the larger channel. [Art Basel / UBS report 2026](https://www.artbasel.com/stories/the-art-basel-and-ubs-global-art-market-report-2026), [The Art Newspaper summary](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2026/03/12/art-basel-ubs-art-market-report-2025-four-percent-growth)
- Dealers' operating costs rose about 5% on average in 2025, faster than their sales. Dealers turning over under $500k grew, while the $500k to $1m middle tier shrank. That middle tier is where cash pressure is worst. [Art Basel / UBS report 2026](https://www.artbasel.com/stories/the-art-basel-and-ubs-global-art-market-report-2026)
- In 2023, primary-market dealers represented 23 artists on average. The top-selling artist brought in about a third of a dealer's sales, and the top three brought in 53%, so one artist's money is often a large share of a gallery's float. [Art Basel / UBS Art Market 2024, Dealers](https://theartmarket.artbasel.com/the-art-market-2024/dealers)
- In the same survey, 40% of dealers were less profitable in 2023 than in 2022, and in 2024, 43% were less profitable than the year before. [Art Market 2024, Dealers](https://theartmarket.artbasel.com/the-art-market-2024/dealers), [Unpacking the 2025 report](https://www.artbasel.com/stories/unpacking-the-art-market-report-art-basel-ubs-2025)
- Galleries are small businesses. A 2014 survey of about 8,000 galleries found 55% had revenue under $200,000 a year. One lawyer quoted in the coverage said suing over sums up to $15,000 isn't worth the legal fees. [The Art Newspaper, 2016](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2016/10/14/collector-and-lawyer-peter-hort-can-pay-will-pay-artists)
- The standard primary split is 50/50. Galleries usually ask for the right to discount up to 10%, with the discount shared. Above 10%, the gallery should get the artist's approval or bear the extra itself. Museum discounts of 20% are sometimes shared too. Artists are typically paid 30 to 60 days after the gallery receives the buyer's money. [The Creative Independent](https://thecreativeindependent.com/guides/how-to-work-with-galleries-and-collectors-as-an-emerging-artist/)
- Who bears a discount varies by dealer. Some absorb it entirely, some share up to 10% and absorb the rest, and some share up to 20%. Collector discounts range from 5% to 50% or more. [Glasstire archive](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/glasstire/20130116074023/http://glasstire.com/2012/11/25/artists-galleries-and-collectors-how-the-business-of-the-art-world-works-sometimes), [The Art Newspaper, 2020](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2020/12/22/gallery-asks-collectors-to-give-their-discounts-back-to-the-artists)
- Installment buying is common and often structured. Art Money finances $1,000 to $50,000 purchases over 10 equal interest-free monthly payments. Many galleries run their own layaway plans. [Newswire: Art Money partnership](https://www.newswire.com/news/art-gallery-pure-partners-with-art-money-to-offer-artwork-financing-21126891), [The Stranger](https://www.thestranger.com/?p=261898)
- Illinois law says installment money goes first to the balance due the artist, unless a clear written agreement sets a percentage split for each installment. Artists must also be paid within 30 days of the dealer receiving the funds, unless agreed otherwise in writing. [815 ILCS 320/2 (FindLaw)](https://codes.findlaw.com/il/chapter-815-business-transactions/il-st-sect-815-320-2/)
- California and Minnesota say consignment proceeds are trust funds that "first be applied to pay any balance due" to the consignor, unless the consignor agrees otherwise in writing. California also puts consigned works beyond the dealer's creditors. [Cal. Civ. Code 1738.6](https://california.public.law/codes/ca_civ_code_section_1738.6), [Minn. Stat. 324.03](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/2007/cite/324.03)
- New York §12.01 makes consigned works and their proceeds trust property that the merchant's creditors can't reach. It applies trustee standards from EPTL 11-1.6. An artist can waive the proceeds trust only in a signed, conspicuous writing, and the waiver never covers the first $2,500 of gross sales in a 12-month period. [NY Arts and Cultural Affairs Law §12.01](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ACA/12.01)
- Stephen Friedman Gallery's administration filings show about £7.8m of debt. More than £800k is owed to artists, including Diop £341,905, Roberts £289,232 and Wiley £163,849. Coutts is secured for £3.1m and expects about 65% back, while unsecured creditors expect 8 to 9 pence per pound. [Summary of The Art Newspaper report](https://usaartnews.com/news/full-extent-of-stephen-friedman-gallerys-7-8m-debt-revealed-in-filings-the-art-newspaper-international-art-news-and-events)
- In England, a trust claim over sale proceeds is hard to win when the money was mixed into the gallery's own account. Artists who were paid before an insolvency can also face clawback claims as "preferences". One 2024 London collapse produced many such claims against artists. [Withers, via Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/united-states/litigation-mediation-arbitration/gallery-closures-protecting-artists)
- 2025 closures included Blum, Kasmin, Clearing, Venus Over Manhattan, Tilton and Sperone Westwater, mostly blamed on rising costs and weak sales. Yet New York still recorded 36 openings against 20 closures that year, so closures make headlines out of proportion to their number. [Art Basel 2025 roundup](https://www.artbasel.com/stories/art-market-2025-roundup), [Art Basel, NYC galleries](https://www.artbasel.com/stories/art-market-report-2026-galleries-pop-up-collaboration-new-york-city)
- The UK resale royalty is a cumulative sliding scale on the resale price: 4% to £50k, 3% to £200k, 1% to £350k, 0.5% to £500k and 0.25% above that. It applies from £1,000 and is capped at £12,500 per sale. [GOV.UK Artist's Resale Right](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/artists-resale-right)
- Escrow for art sales has been tried before. RISBE held the buyer's money and released each party's share once it cleared, for up to 2% a transaction, but galleries had to opt in. [The Art Newspaper, 2016](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2016/10/14/collector-and-lawyer-peter-hort-can-pay-will-pay-artists)

## How it applies to Rescrow

- The current split is a flat percentage of each payment. Illinois and California both default to "artist first": installment money pays down the artist's balance before the gallery takes anything. Rescrow can model that as a rule the agreement selects, and the agent can cite the clause that picks it.
- Discounts are where artists quietly lose money. The seed agreements already contain "up to 10%, shared equally" and "above 10% needs written approval" clauses, but the PRD lists discount sharing as cut. It is a cheap, high-fidelity rule to bring back.
- Small galleries with rising costs and thin margins are the users most tempted to use artist money as working capital. A 30 to 60 day payout norm means a large float of artist money sits in operating at any moment, and Rescrow removes that float.
- The Stephen Friedman filings let us show real numbers on screen: £800k owed to artists, 8 to 9p recovery, and a secured bank taking 65% first.
- The English clawback risk is a second failure mode beyond lost money: artists can be asked to give back payments they received. Payouts that provably come from a segregated trust hold are the answer.
- Payments in the demo are bare amounts. Real buyer payments carry sales tax, and sometimes deposits and forfeitures. Each needs a home in reconcile() so the equation keeps balancing to the cent.

## Feature ideas

### Discount allocation engine with an approval gate

- What: Add `discountCents` and `discountApproval?: {by, at, quote}` to Sale, plus agreement terms `sharedDiscountCapBps` (e.g. 1000) and `discountBearer: "shared" | "gallery"`. The artist's share is computed on the list price minus only the shared part of the discount. Any discount above the cap is charged entirely to the gallery's share unless an approval record exists. A pure function `allocateDiscount(list, sold, terms)` returns `{artistCents, galleryCents, galleryAbsorbedCents}`, with property tests that the parts always add up to the sale price.
- Why judges care: it is a real rule from the seed agreements, enforced to the cent, and it is where artists lose money without noticing.
- Demo visibility: both
- Effort: M
- Demo risk: low. It is a pure function. The sale card shows one extra line, "gallery absorbed $1,200 of a 15% discount".
- Grounding: [The Creative Independent](https://thecreativeindependent.com/guides/how-to-work-with-galleries-and-collectors-as-an-emerging-artist/), [Glasstire archive](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/glasstire/20130116074023/http://glasstire.com/2012/11/25/artists-galleries-and-collectors-how-the-business-of-the-art-world-works-sometimes), seed agreements in `src/lib/seed.ts`

### Artist-first vs pro-rata installment allocation

- What: Add `allocation: "pro_rata" | "artist_first"` to AgreementTerms. Under artist_first, each payment fills the artist's remaining balance before the gallery gets anything, so on a $24,000 sale at 50/50, payment 1 of $8,000 goes entirely to trust. The agent defaults to artist_first, which is the Illinois and California default, unless it finds and cites a clause that sets a per-installment percentage. The present page shows two stacked bars per payment: the actual allocation and what the other policy would have done.
- Why judges care: it is a statutory default turned into an allocation algorithm, and it makes the insolvency beat stronger, because more of the artist's money is already in trust when the gallery fails.
- Demo visibility: both
- Effort: M
- Demo risk: low. It changes hold amounts but not the hold or release paths. Running reconcile after every operation in tests covers it.
- Grounding: [815 ILCS 320/2](https://codes.findlaw.com/il/chapter-815-business-transactions/il-st-sect-815-320-2/), [Cal. Civ. Code 1738.6(d)](https://california.public.law/codes/ca_civ_code_section_1738.6)

### Sales tax carve-out in every payment

- What: Add a gallery tax rate stored as an integer in hundredths of a basis point (NYC's 8.875% is 88750) and a `taxCents` field on IncomingPayment. The split runs on the pre-tax amount, and tax goes to a `taxPayable` bucket that is neither trust nor operating. reconcile() becomes opening + received = operating + trust + paid + refunded + claimed + taxPayable. A test checks that tax is never split to the artist and never reaches creditors as gallery cash.
- Why judges care: it is real payment fidelity that a finance engineer will notice. The artist's 50% is of the price, not of the tax.
- Demo visibility: repo-rigor
- Effort: M
- Demo risk: med. It touches the reconcile equation and the seed numbers, so the demo script figures must be rechecked.
- Grounding: from the codebase (`reconcile()`, `IncomingPayment`). The pre-tax royalty basis in [GOV.UK ARR](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/artists-resale-right) is a parallel case.

### Price waterfall card with a citation on every line

- What: For each sale, one card lists list price, discount (shared or absorbed), gallery commission, deductions (framing, capped), and artist net. Each line links to the agreement quote that justifies it, using the existing citations. The numbers come from one pure `waterfall(sale, terms)` function that the statement and the UI both use, so they cannot disagree.
- Why judges care: it shows the full economics of a consignment in one glance, and every cent can be traced to a clause.
- Demo visibility: shown-on-screen
- Effort: S
- Demo risk: low. It is a read-only view over existing data.
- Grounding: [The Creative Independent](https://thecreativeindependent.com/guides/how-to-work-with-galleries-and-collectors-as-an-emerging-artist/), from the codebase (citations, Theo framing clause)

### Payment plan generator with cent-exact schedule

- What: `planInstallments(priceCents, {depositBps, count, startDate, intervalDays})` returns the due dates and amounts. The remainder goes on the last payment, so the amounts sum exactly to the price. Presets cover 3 equal payments, 10% deposit plus 9 monthly, and 10 equal monthly. Each expected payment gets `expectedBy`. An incoming payment matches to the earliest unpaid line, and lines past due turn amber on the present page.
- Why judges care: real buyer behavior, modeled precisely, with rounding handled the same way as the D5 running totals.
- Demo visibility: both
- Effort: M
- Demo risk: low. It is additive, and the existing three-payment demo is just one preset.
- Grounding: [Newswire: Art Money](https://www.newswire.com/news/art-gallery-pure-partners-with-art-money-to-offer-artwork-financing-21126891), [The Stranger](https://www.thestranger.com/?p=261898)

### Buyer default and forfeited deposit

- What: If a payment is more than N grace days past `expectedBy`, the gallery can mark the sale defaulted. The agreement's `forfeiture` term decides what happens to the money already paid: it is either refunded, or kept and split like sale proceeds. Under "kept", the artist's held share pays out to the artist instead of returning to the buyer, still only through releaseTrust(to: artist|buyer). The work goes back to `consigned`.
- Why judges care: it covers a messy real-world case without opening any new way out of trust.
- Demo visibility: both
- Effort: M
- Demo risk: med. It needs a clean separation from the existing cancel path, and that needs tests.
- Grounding: [The Creative Independent](https://thecreativeindependent.com/guides/how-to-work-with-galleries-and-collectors-as-an-emerging-artist/) (non-refundable deposits), from the codebase (cancel path, releaseTrust)

### Payout provenance for clawback defense

- What: Every Payout already lists `holdIds`. Add a check, `provePayoutSources()`, confirming each payout cent traces to a hold funded by a specific incoming payment for that artist's sale, and that no payout drew on operating cash. After insolvency, the screen shows "N of N payouts came from trust funds, 0 from the gallery's own cash", with each payout linked to its trace.
- Why judges care: it answers the second insolvency risk, clawback of payments already made, with an invariant instead of a legal argument.
- Demo visibility: both
- Effort: S
- Demo risk: low. It only reads data that already exists.
- Grounding: [Withers, via Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/united-states/litigation-mediation-arbitration/gallery-closures-protecting-artists)

### Artist float calculator ("money sitting in operating")

- What: A small deterministic panel. Its inputs are gallery turnover, artist share and average days to pay, and it computes the artist money held in a commingled account at any time: annual artist proceeds × days ÷ 365. A $1m gallery at 50% and 60 days holds about $82k of artist money in operating. Under Rescrow the same line reads $0 in operating and $X in trust.
- Why judges care: it frames the problem in treasury terms, as float, which is language Ramp engineers use.
- Demo visibility: shown-on-screen
- Effort: S
- Demo risk: low. It is a pure formula, and its inputs are labeled as illustrative.
- Grounding: [The Creative Independent](https://thecreativeindependent.com/guides/how-to-work-with-galleries-and-collectors-as-an-emerging-artist/) (30 to 60 days), [Art Basel / UBS report 2026](https://www.artbasel.com/stories/the-art-basel-and-ubs-global-art-market-report-2026) (turnover segments)

### Creditor waterfall with the real Stephen Friedman numbers

- What: A fixed scenario loaded from the published filing figures: £7.8m debt, £3.1m secured at 65%, more than £800k to artists, and 8 to 9p for unsecured creditors. It runs through two pure functions, a commingled waterfall where artists rank unsecured and the Rescrow model where trust sits outside the estate, and shows each artist's recovery side by side. It complements the existing "without Rescrow" counterfactual idea by using the hook's real case instead of the demo gallery.
- Why judges care: it ties the hook to arithmetic. The video can say "Wiley gets about £14k back instead of £163,849".
- Demo visibility: shown-on-screen
- Effort: M
- Demo risk: low. The data is static. The figures must be labeled as reported estimates, not outcomes.
- Grounding: [Summary of The Art Newspaper report](https://usaartnews.com/news/full-extent-of-stephen-friedman-gallerys-7-8m-debt-revealed-in-filings-the-art-newspaper-international-art-news-and-events), [Withers, via Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/united-states/litigation-mediation-arbitration/gallery-closures-protecting-artists)

### Concentration exposure panel

- What: For each artist, show their trust balance, their share of the gallery's sales, and their share of total trust. Flag the case where one artist is more than a third of sales, which is the industry average for a top artist. Seed a second and third artist so the bar chart has shape.
- Why judges care: it shows who would be hurt most if the gallery failed, and that Rescrow protects each of them individually.
- Demo visibility: shown-on-screen
- Effort: S
- Demo risk: low. It is read-only aggregation over holds and sales.
- Grounding: [Art Basel / UBS Art Market 2024, Dealers](https://theartmarket.artbasel.com/the-art-market-2024/dealers)

### Resale royalty calculator for secondary sales

- What: A pure `resaleRoyalty(pricePence)` that implements the UK cumulative bands, the £1,000 threshold and the £12,500 cap in integer pence. Boundary tests run at £50,000.00 and £50,000.01, and at the cap. On a secondary sale, the royalty is held in trust for the artist or the collecting society, alongside the consignor's share, and released only through releaseTrust.
- Why judges care: it is a tiered, capped, rounding-sensitive calculation, the kind fintech engineers test with tables, and it widens Rescrow to secondary sales.
- Demo visibility: repo-rigor
- Effort: M
- Demo risk: low. It is isolated, and secondary sales can stay off the main demo path.
- Grounding: [GOV.UK Artist's Resale Right](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/artists-resale-right)

### Net-price secondary consignment

- What: Add `split: {kind: "net_price", consignorNetCents}` as an alternative to a percentage. The consignor's fixed net fills trust first from each installment. Only money above the net reaches operating, and the consignor's statement shows the final sale price, so the margin is visible to them.
- Why judges care: it handles the other common deal structure, and visibility of the price closes a known abuse, where a dealer quietly keeps the difference.
- Demo visibility: repo-rigor
- Effort: M
- Demo risk: low. The split function gains a second kind, and reconcile stays unchanged.
- Grounding: [The Art Newspaper, 2016](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2016/10/14/collector-and-lawyer-peter-hort-can-pay-will-pay-artists)

### Statutory default payout window when the agreement is silent

- What: If the agent finds no payout clause, `payoutDays` comes from a small jurisdiction table instead of a guess. Illinois, for example, defaults to 30 days. The source is marked `"statute"` with the citation, and agreements whose window is longer than 60 days are flagged as above the industry norm.
- Why judges care: it never makes up a number, and it shows exactly where each number came from.
- Demo visibility: both
- Effort: S
- Demo risk: low. It is a table lookup on the fixture path.
- Grounding: [815 ILCS 320/2](https://codes.findlaw.com/il/chapter-815-business-transactions/il-st-sect-815-320-2/), [The Creative Independent](https://thecreativeindependent.com/guides/how-to-work-with-galleries-and-collectors-as-an-emerging-artist/)
