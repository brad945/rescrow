# US artist-dealer consignment statutes and what Rescrow can enforce

Summaries below come from unofficial code sites (public.law, FindLaw, Justia) and law-firm or nonprofit commentary. Some pages are older code versions. Check the official text before relying on any detail. None of this is legal advice.

## What we learned

- **New York 12.01 creates the consignment by law, not by contract.** When an artist (or craftsperson, or their successor) delivers work to an art merchant for sale on commission, a consignor/consignee relationship arises "notwithstanding" trade custom, the UCC, or a contrary agreement. The dealer is the artist's agent, the work is trust property, and sale proceeds are trust funds. ([NY ACAL 12.01, public.law](https://newyork.public.law/laws/n.y._arts_and_cultural_affairs_law_section_12.01))
- **New York: trust money never becomes the gallery's.** 12.01(1)(a)(v) says trust property and trust funds are a statutory trust, do not become the consignee's property, and are not subject to its creditors' claims, liens or security interests. ([NY ACAL 12.01, public.law](https://newyork.public.law/laws/n.y._arts_and_cultural_affairs_law_section_12.01))
- **New York's separation duty comes through the trust law, not 12.01 itself.** 12.01(2) says a consignee that fails to treat trust funds per EPTL 11-1.6 violates both laws. EPTL 11-1.6 requires a fiduciary to keep fiduciary property separate from its own, never deposit it in its own name, and hold it "in his name as fiduciary." A violation is a misdemeanor. ([NY ACAL 12.01](https://newyork.public.law/laws/n.y._arts_and_cultural_affairs_law_section_12.01), [NY EPTL 11-1.6, public.law](https://newyork.public.law/laws/n.y._estates,_powers_and_trusts_law_section_11-1.6))
- **These rules came after Salander-O'Reilly.** The 2012 amendments were a response to dealers not keeping consignor proceeds apart from their operating accounts. Commentators say commingling now carries criminal and civil exposure. ([Center for Art Law, 2012](https://itsartlaw.org/2012/11/09/cave-consignor-ny-art-consignment-law-muscles-up/))
- **New York waivers are narrow.** Waiving any part of 12.01 is "absolutely void" except the trust-funds clause. A waiver of that clause must be clear, conspicuous, written and signed. It still cannot reach the first $2,500 of gross proceeds in any 12-month period after signing, or proceeds of a work the gallery bought for itself, and it cannot help the gallery's creditors against the artist. ([NY ACAL 12.01(1)(b)](https://newyork.public.law/laws/n.y._arts_and_cultural_affairs_law_section_12.01))
- **If the gallery buys the work, the trust survives.** In New York the work stays trust property until the price is paid in full to the artist. If the gallery resells it first, the resale proceeds are trust funds up to the unpaid balance. ([NY ACAL 12.01(1)(a)(iv)](https://newyork.public.law/laws/n.y._arts_and_cultural_affairs_law_section_12.01), [Center for Art Law](https://itsartlaw.org/2012/11/09/cave-consignor-ny-art-consignment-law-muscles-up/))
- **New York sets no payment deadline, no accounting schedule and no records rule.** Payment timing is left to the agreement, which is why Rescrow reads `payoutDays` from the contract. ([NY ACAL 12.01, public.law](https://newyork.public.law/laws/n.y._arts_and_cultural_affairs_law_section_12.01))
- **California: proceeds are trust funds and pay the artist first.** Civil Code 1738.6(b) puts the work in trust and out of reach of the dealer's creditors. 1738.6(d) makes proceeds trust funds that are "first applied to pay any balance due" the artist unless the artist agrees otherwise in writing. 1738.6(c) makes the dealer responsible for loss or damage. 1738.6 sets no payment deadline. ([Cal. Civ. Code 1738.6, public.law](https://california.public.law/codes/ca_civ_code_section_1738.6))
- **California voids all waivers.** Section 1738.8 voids any contract term by which the artist waives the chapter. A 2026 write-up describes a court applying this to a fire loss of consigned paintings. ([Carlton Fields](https://www.carltonfields.com/insights/publications/2026/for-fine-art-transactions-its-best-to-read-the-fine-print), [Cal Globe](https://californiaglobe.com/fr/consigning-fine-art/))
- **Illinois, Washington and Oregon default to 30 days.** Trust funds must reach the artist within 30 days of the dealer receiving them unless the parties agree otherwise in writing. ([815 ILCS 320/2, FindLaw](https://codes.findlaw.com/il/chapter-815-business-transactions/il-st-sect-815-320-2/), [RCW 18.110](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=18.110&full=true), [ORS 359.225](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_359.225))
- **Installments go to the artist's balance first in Washington and Oregon.** Each installment first pays down what the artist is owed on the sale. A proportional split of each installment is allowed only if the artist agrees to it in writing. ([ORS 359.225](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_359.225), [RCW 18.110.020](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=18.110&full=true))
- **Illinois treats customer deposits separately.** The 30-day clock for a deposit starts when the deposit "becomes part of the payment" for the work, not when it lands. ([815 ILCS 320/2, FindLaw](https://codes.findlaw.com/il/chapter-815-business-transactions/il-st-sect-815-320-2/))
- **Several states require a written contract with set fields.** Illinois requires one before acceptance or within seven days, stating value, payment time, commission, minimum price and usual discounts. Washington and Oregon require value, minimum price and commission, and Oregon adds payment time. Washington and Illinois also require telling viewers the work is the artist's when it is displayed. ([815 ILCS 320/5, FindLaw](https://codes.findlaw.com/il/chapter-815-business-transactions/il-st-sect-815-320-5/), [RCW 18.110.030](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=18.110&full=true), [Oregon VLA](https://www.oregonvla.org/resources/fine-art-consignments))
- **Oregon has the strongest penalties.** Knowingly withholding or taking an artist's proceeds is a Class C felony under ORS 359.240. On written demand, the dealer must give the buyer's name, address, date and price for any sale of $100 or more, or owe treble damages. ([ORS 359.240](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_359.240), [ORS 359.210](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_359.210))
- **The protection is broad in theory and thin in practice.** One 2019 survey counts 31 states with artist-consignor statutes, notes that collector-consignors lack that protection, and says creditors were repaid ahead of consignors in the Berry-Hill, Salander-O'Reilly and Ace Gallery failures. ([Center for Art Law, 2019](https://itsartlaw.org/2019/12/05/the-art-of-bankruptcy-consigned-artworks-and-bankrupt-galleries/))
- **After a dealer fails, recovery is slow and partial.** Works get appraised, title disputes are flagged, and assets sell at liquidation value. In one case 108 works were listed as missing. Resolution can take years. ([The Art Newspaper, 2023](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2023/10/02/when-art-dealers-go-bust-what-happens-to-the-art-they-hold))

## How it applies to Rescrow

- **Correct one pitch line.** The PRD and demo script say "nothing makes the gallery keep it separate." In New York, EPTL 11-1.6 (via 12.01(2)) does require separation, under a misdemeanor penalty. The honest gap is that nothing enforces it or proves it at the moment of each payment. Rescrow does both: `releaseTrust` is the only exit, and `reconcile()` is the proof. Suggested wording: "The law says keep it separate. Nothing makes them, and nobody checks until it's too late."
- **Rescrow's split is pro rata. Several states default to artist-first.** `artistShareOfPayment` gives the artist 50% of every installment. Under WA and OR, and arguably CA 1738.6(d), installments pay the artist's balance first unless the artist agreed in writing to a proportional split. The Lowline gallery is in New York, which has no installment rule, so the current behaviour is lawful there. A per-jurisdiction mode would make the engine follow the law of wherever the gallery sits.
- **The trust account is already structured the way EPTL 11-1.6 describes.** Separate, titled as fiduciary, never in the gallery's own name. Naming the account that way on screen ("Lowline Gallery LLC, as trustee for consignors") ties the UI to the statute.
- **`dueBy` matches the 30-day default states.** A per-payment deadline from receipt is how IL, WA and OR phrase it. Where an agreement gives no period, the engine could fall back to the state default instead of trusting a missing value.
- **The insolvency beat is the statute in code.** NY 12.01(1)(a)(v), CA 1738.6(b) and ORS 359.225 all say creditors cannot reach the artist's share. The trust guard refusing creditors is a direct implementation, and Oregon's felony for withholding proceeds is good context for why a hard refusal (not a warning) is the right design.

## Feature ideas

### Artist-first installment allocation

- What: Add `allocation: "artist_first" | "pro_rata"` to the sale (taken from the agreement, defaulting by jurisdiction). Artist-first puts `min(payment, artistTotal - artistPaidSoFar)` into trust, and the rest goes to operating. Pro rata keeps the current running-total split, and is allowed in WA/OR only when the agreement has a cited clause agreeing to it. Mara's agreement ("Artist's share of each installment") is arguably that clause. Ines's agreement has none.
- Why judges care: It's a real waterfall rule from statute, exact in integer cents, with no rounding at all on the artist-first path.
- Demo visibility: both
- Effort: M (1-3h)
- Demo risk: med. The narration says "half" goes to trust on each wire, so the demo gallery must stay on pro rata (New York) or the script must change. Show artist-first as a toggle or a second gallery.
- Grounding: [ORS 359.225](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_359.225), [RCW 18.110.020](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=18.110&full=true), [Cal. Civ. Code 1738.6(d)](https://california.public.law/codes/ca_civ_code_section_1738.6); `artistShareOfPayment` in src/lib/escrow/engine.ts

### Jurisdiction profiles

- What: A typed, frozen table `JURISDICTIONS: Record<"NY"|"CA"|"IL"|"WA"|"OR", Profile>` with fields like `defaultPayoutDays` (30, or null for NY/CA), `installmentDefault`, `waiverAllowed` (NY: trust-funds clause only; CA/IL/WA/OR: never), `requiredContractFields`, and `citation`. The gallery gets a `jurisdiction` field, and engine rules read from the profile instead of hard-coded constants. A small "Governing law" card in the UI shows the active profile with its statute links.
- Why judges care: Real-world fidelity, and it shows the rules are data with tests, not scattered `if` statements.
- Demo visibility: both
- Effort: M (1-3h)
- Demo risk: low. The demo stays NY. The profile is just displayed.
- Grounding: all statute links above

### Statutory payout deadline fallback

- What: If the agreement reader returns no `payoutDays`, or a value it can't back with a verified citation, `dueBy` uses the jurisdiction default (30 days in IL/WA/OR). Where the state has no default (NY/CA), the hold is flagged "needs review" instead of a guess being used. The hold shows where its deadline came from: "Agreement clause 4" or "ORS 359.225 default".
- Why judges care: It fails safe. A missing number never quietly becomes "never pay".
- Demo visibility: both
- Effort: S (<1h)
- Demo risk: low. Seeded agreements all state a period, so the fallback only appears in tests or a second fixture.
- Grounding: [815 ILCS 320/2](https://codes.findlaw.com/il/chapter-815-business-transactions/il-st-sect-815-320-2/), [ORS 359.225](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_359.225), [RCW 18.110](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=18.110&full=true)

### Deadline cap: agreement cannot exceed the law without a written clause

- What: In 30-day states, an extracted `payoutDays` above 30 is accepted only if its citation quote is verified in the agreement text (the existing exact-match check). Otherwise the engine clamps to 30 and records a `VerificationCheck` that says why. A test fixture with "payment within 90 days" but a broken quote shows the clamp.
- Why judges care: It ties the existing citation check to a legal consequence instead of a confidence badge.
- Demo visibility: repo-rigor
- Effort: S (<1h)
- Demo risk: low. Not on the main path.
- Grounding: [RCW 18.110.020](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=18.110&full=true), [815 ILCS 320/2](https://codes.findlaw.com/il/chapter-815-business-transactions/il-st-sect-815-320-2/); `src/lib/agent/verify.ts`

### New York waiver floor ($2,500 per 12 months)

- What: Model an optional `TrustWaiver { signedOn, signedByArtist, conspicuous }` on the agreement. Even with a valid waiver, the first $2,500 of gross proceeds in each 12-month window still goes to trust. The window is anchored at the signing date, which is our reading of "after the execution of the waiver". Proceeds of works the gallery bought for itself never qualify. A pure function `waivableCents(history, waiver, payment)` with property-based tests: the trust never gets less than the floor, whatever the order and size of payments.
- Why judges care: It's a tricky windowed accumulation rule, exactly the sort of edge case fintech engineers test with properties.
- Demo visibility: repo-rigor
- Effort: M (1-3h)
- Demo risk: low. Engine-only. No seeded agreement has a waiver.
- Grounding: [NY ACAL 12.01(1)(b)](https://newyork.public.law/laws/n.y._arts_and_cultural_affairs_law_section_12.01)

### Waiver lint: void clauses are flagged and ignored

- What: The agreement reader's saved answers can include clauses like "Artist waives trust protection". A deterministic rule checks each against the jurisdiction: void in CA/IL/WA (and for everything except the trust-funds clause in NY), so the clause is shown struck through with "void under Cal. Civ. Code 1738.8" and has no effect on the split. A fixture agreement has such a clause.
- Why judges care: The software refuses a bad contract term instead of executing it.
- Demo visibility: shown-on-screen
- Effort: S (<1h)
- Demo risk: low. It's a static highlight on an existing agreement view.
- Grounding: [Cal Globe on 1738.8](https://californiaglobe.com/fr/consigning-fine-art/), [RCW 18.110.030](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=18.110&full=true), [NY ACAL 12.01(1)(b)](https://newyork.public.law/laws/n.y._arts_and_cultural_affairs_law_section_12.01)

### Gallery buys the work: trust survives until paid in full

- What: A sale whose buyer is the gallery itself (`buyerKind: "consignee"`) keeps the artwork in a `trust_until_paid` state. If the gallery resells it before paying the artist, `receivePayment` routes the resale proceeds into trust up to the artist's unpaid balance first. Any NY waiver is ignored for this sale. An invariant test checks that the artist's unpaid balance is always covered by trust holds or by the artwork still being in trust.
- Why judges care: It closes the most obvious loophole: "we'll just buy it ourselves and pay later".
- Demo visibility: repo-rigor
- Effort: M (1-3h)
- Demo risk: low. Not in the scripted demo.
- Grounding: [NY ACAL 12.01(1)(a)(iv)](https://newyork.public.law/laws/n.y._arts_and_cultural_affairs_law_section_12.01), [Center for Art Law, 2012](https://itsartlaw.org/2012/11/09/cave-consignor-ny-art-consignment-law-muscles-up/)

### Contract completeness check before consignment is accepted

- What: Before the artwork can be marked `consigned`, a pure check verifies the agreement has the fields the profile requires (Illinois: value, payment time, commission, minimum price and usual discounts, signed within 7 days of acceptance). Each field shows a green tick or a red gap with the statute line. Missing fields block `createSale` in states that void the dealer's commission without a contract.
- Why judges care: A compliance checklist driven by data, done at intake rather than discovered in litigation.
- Demo visibility: shown-on-screen
- Effort: M (1-3h)
- Demo risk: med. Seeded agreements may lack a minimum price, so the NY demo should show this as informational only.
- Grounding: [815 ILCS 320/5](https://codes.findlaw.com/il/chapter-815-business-transactions/il-st-sect-815-320-5/), [RCW 18.110.030](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=18.110&full=true), [Oregon VLA](https://www.oregonvla.org/resources/fine-art-consignments)

### Minimum price and discount guard

- What: Add `minimumPriceCents` and `maxDiscountBps` to the agreement terms. `createSale` refuses a price below the minimum, or a discount above the agreed cap, unless an `artistApproval` record is attached. Ines's agreement already caps discounts at 10% without written approval, so the rule has a cited clause in the seed.
- Why judges care: It's the same "code, not policy" stance as the trust guard, applied to the sale's price.
- Demo visibility: both
- Effort: S (<1h)
- Demo risk: low. The demo sale is at full price.
- Grounding: [815 ILCS 320/5](https://codes.findlaw.com/il/chapter-815-business-transactions/il-st-sect-815-320-5/), [RCW 18.110.030](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=18.110&full=true); Ines agreement clause 2 in src/lib/seed.ts

### Customer deposits start the clock late (Illinois rule)

- What: Mark an incoming payment as `kind: "deposit"` while the sale is still conditional (for example, on approval). It still goes to trust immediately, but its `dueBy` is computed from `appliedAt`, the moment the deposit becomes part of the payment, not from `receivedAt`. If the sale is cancelled, the deposit is refunded from trust through the existing `releaseTrust(to: buyer)` path.
- Why judges care: It shows the team understands that "received" and "earned" are different timestamps.
- Demo visibility: repo-rigor
- Effort: M (1-3h)
- Demo risk: low. Engine plus tests only.
- Grounding: [815 ILCS 320/2](https://codes.findlaw.com/il/chapter-815-business-transactions/il-st-sect-815-320-2/)

### Fiduciary account title and "statutory trust" label

- What: Show the trust account as "Lowline Gallery LLC, as trustee for consignors" with a small caption citing EPTL 11-1.6. Each hold row gets a short legal basis line, for example "Trust funds, NY ACAL 12.01(1)(a)(iii)". This is a copy and data change, a `legalBasis` string on the jurisdiction profile.
- Why judges care: It's a cheap detail that tells a judge the team read the actual law.
- Demo visibility: shown-on-screen
- Effort: S (<1h)
- Demo risk: low. Static text.
- Grounding: [NY EPTL 11-1.6](https://newyork.public.law/laws/n.y._estates,_powers_and_trusts_law_section_11-1.6), [NY ACAL 12.01](https://newyork.public.law/laws/n.y._arts_and_cultural_affairs_law_section_12.01)

### Insolvency "statute card": why the creditors get nothing from trust

- What: When `POST /api/demo/insolvency` runs, the refused creditor move is shown alongside the governing rule: "not subject to claims of creditors", NY 12.01(1)(a)(v). It also shows the counterfactual: in a commingled account, the artist would sit in line with unsecured creditors (the PRD's 8-9p per pound figure). Both numbers come from the same snapshot, so the card reconciles.
- Why judges care: The demo's climax gets a citation, not just a red error.
- Demo visibility: shown-on-screen
- Effort: S (<1h)
- Demo risk: low. It's an overlay on an existing beat. Keep it to one line so it doesn't eat time.
- Grounding: [NY ACAL 12.01](https://newyork.public.law/laws/n.y._arts_and_cultural_affairs_law_section_12.01), [Center for Art Law, 2019](https://itsartlaw.org/2019/12/05/the-art-of-bankruptcy-consigned-artworks-and-bankrupt-galleries/); from the codebase (`declareInsolvency`)

### Buyer disclosure on demand (Oregon, sales of $100 or more)

- What: Add an artist-portal button, "Request buyer details", that records a `DisclosureRequest` with a timestamp. The gallery side shows a countdown and a one-click "send buyer name, date, price". Sales under $100 are excluded by rule. The record is append-only, so an ignored request is visible evidence.
- Why judges care: It's a small, auditable workflow that turns a statutory right into a button.
- Demo visibility: shown-on-screen
- Effort: M (1-3h)
- Demo risk: low. Optional beat.
- Grounding: [ORS 359.210](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_359.210)

### Compliance invariants in the test suite

- What: Add `compliance.test.ts`, which runs randomized payment, cancel, insolvency and payout sequences and checks statute-derived properties after every step. No hold outlives its statutory deadline. The trust balance always covers the unpaid artist share. No trust cent ever reaches operating or creditors. Each property is named after its source line, for example `"ORS 359.225: artist paid within 30 days"`.
- Why judges care: Tests named after the law read like a spec, and they run in CI.
- Demo visibility: repo-rigor
- Effort: M (1-3h)
- Demo risk: low. Tests only.
- Grounding: [ORS 359.225](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_359.225), [NY ACAL 12.01](https://newyork.public.law/laws/n.y._arts_and_cultural_affairs_law_section_12.01); `src/lib/escrow/invariants.test.ts`
