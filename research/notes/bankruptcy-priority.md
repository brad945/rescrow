# What happens to money when a business goes bankrupt: chapter 7 vs 11, priority, tracing and clawbacks

Scope: US bankruptcy law and tracing case law, with English cases marked where they appear. Stephen Friedman Gallery is a UK administration, not a US chapter 7 or 11 case.

## What we learned

- **Chapter 7 liquidates; chapter 11 keeps the business running.** In chapter 7 a trustee collects and sells the debtor's nonexempt property for creditors, can avoid preferences made in the 90 days before filing, and can run the business only briefly. A corporation gets no chapter 7 discharge. Filing also triggers an automatic stay, which stops most collection actions without a court order. [uscourts.gov, Chapter 7 basics](https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-7-bankruptcy-basics)
- **In chapter 11 the debtor usually stays in charge as "debtor in possession", with a trustee's duties.** A chapter 11 trustee is appointed only for cause such as fraud or gross mismanagement. The debtor must file monthly operating reports and may use cash collateral only with consent or court approval. A case can be converted to chapter 7 for cause, including continuing losses. [uscourts.gov, Chapter 11 basics](https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-11-bankruptcy-basics)
- **Payouts follow a strict class order.** Under §726, priority claims (§507) come first, then timely unsecured claims, then late claims, then fines and penalties, then interest, and the debtor gets any surplus last. Each class is paid in full before the next gets anything. Within a class that cannot be paid in full, claims share pro rata. [Cornell LII, 11 U.S.C. §726](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/726)
- **Within the priority tier, §507 sets ten ranks.** Support obligations rank first and administrative expenses second. Wages earned in the 180 days before filing rank fourth, capped at $17,150 per person from April 2025, and certain taxes rank eighth. [Cornell LII, 11 U.S.C. §507](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/507)
- **Property the debtor holds only in trust stays out of the estate.** Section 541(d) excludes property in which the debtor holds legal title but no equitable interest. Whether such an interest exists is a question of state law. [ABI, §541(d) opinions index](https://opinions.abi.org/11-usc/%C2%A7-541d), [Trust funds and property of the estate (WCS Law)](https://www.wcslaw.com/accolades/trust-funds-and-property-of-the-estates-in-bankruptcy/)
- **New York's 2012 amendment says this outright for art.** Consigned works and their proceeds are a statutory trust. Proceeds must be deposited in the dealer's name as fiduciary, never in the dealer's own account. They are not part of the dealer's bankruptcy estate and are not subordinate to creditors' liens. A consignor can waive trust treatment only above the first $2,500 a year, and only in writing. The Berry-Hill and Salander-O'Reilly failures drove the change. [Legal 500, NY's revised artist consignment statute](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/united-kingdom/media-telecoms-it-entertainment/news-for-art-dealers-artists-and-artists%E2%80%99-estates-new-york%E2%80%99s-revised-artist-consignment-statute)
- **Commingled trust money is traced with the lowest intermediate balance rule (LIBR).** The claimant recovers no more than the lowest balance the mixed account reached after the trust money went in. Later deposits of the debtor's own money do not refill the trust. In the Tenth Circuit's worked example, $10,000 of trust money is mixed with $10,000 of the fiduciary's own. After $15,000 is withdrawn and $5,000 redeposited, only $5,000 is recoverable. [Hill v. Kinzler (In re Foster), 10th Cir. 2001](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/us-10th-circuit/1004530.html)
- **LIBR is a fiction that courts may decline to use.** The Tenth Circuit reversed because the lower court had not weighed whether letting one claimant trace would hurt similarly situated creditors. Where the whole account is victims' money, _Cunningham v. Brown_ (the 1924 Ponzi case) held that "equality is equity": everyone shares as a creditor. [Hill v. Kinzler](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/us-10th-circuit/1004530.html), [Cunningham v. Brown, 265 U.S. 1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/265/1)
- **Tracing method is a choice, and the evidence is the account's running balance.** Besides LIBR, courts use FIFO, LIFO and pro rata, and pick whichever is most equitable. In _Health Diagnostic Laboratory_ (Bankr. E.D. Va. 2023), an expert followed $1,085,000 through four commingled accounts transaction by transaction and could trace $569,435. [Patterson Belknap, Navigating asset tracing challenges](https://www.pbwt.com/bankruptcy-update-blog/navigating-asset-tracing-challenges-in-bankruptcy)
- **A preference is a payment the trustee can claw back.** It must be a transfer of the debtor's property to a creditor, on an old debt, while insolvent, within 90 days of filing (one year for insiders), that leaves the creditor better off than in a chapter 7. Insolvency is presumed for the 90 days. Defenses include ordinary course of business and new value. Business-debtor transfers under $8,575 in total (from April 2025) cannot be avoided. [Cornell LII, 11 U.S.C. §547](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/547)
- **Paying out trust money is not a preference, because it was never the debtor's property.** In _Begier v. IRS_ (1990), the Supreme Court held that a company's pre-filing payments of withheld taxes, which it held in trust, could not be clawed back. The trust arose when the money was collected, not when it was segregated. Courts use "reasonable assumptions" and need only some connection between the trust and the funds paid. [Begier v. IRS, 496 U.S. 53](https://chanrobles.com/usa/us_supremecourt/496/53/case.php)
- **That protection weakens when the payment comes out of a mixed operating account.** An IRS memo reads _Begier_ narrowly where the payment went to non-trust debts. The _Wendy's_ line of cases held that a remittance from operating accounts with a negative aggregate balance was paid with after-acquired money and could be avoided. [IRS Chief Counsel memo 200029002](https://www.eitc.irs.gov/pub/irs-wd/0029002.pdf), [Begier commentary (CaseMine)](https://www.casemine.com/commentary/us/begier-trustee-v.-internal-revenue-service:-establishing-the-non-avoidability-of-trust-fund-tax-payments-in-bankruptcy/view)
- **English law has no art-trust statute, so separate accounts carry the weight.** In _Re Kayford_ (1975), customer prepayments moved into a separate account were held on trust and kept from creditors, even though the account was not labelled a trust. Without that step, artists rank as unsecured creditors. Stephen Friedman's artists are expected to get 8 to 9 pence per pound, and English law can also claw back payments made up to two years before insolvency. [Re Kayford Ltd](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Re_Kayford), [Withers, Gallery closures: protecting artists](https://www.withersworldwide.com/en-gb/insight/read/gallery-closures-–-protecting-artists)
- **Real gallery bankruptcies favoured creditors over consignors.** Berry-Hill, Salander-O'Reilly (chapter 11, 2007) and ACE Gallery were contested cases where paying creditors came ahead of artists and collectors. In Salander's case, the estate controlled hundreds of consigned works and many artists' children had to buy their parents' works back. [Center for Art Law, The art of bankruptcy](https://itsartlaw.org/2019/10/24/the-art-of-bankruptcy-consigned-artworks-and-bankrupt-galleries/), [Legal 500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/united-kingdom/media-telecoms-it-entertainment/news-for-art-dealers-artists-and-artists%E2%80%99-estates-new-york%E2%80%99s-revised-artist-consignment-statute)
- **The statute's scope is still disputed.** In the Paddle8 bankruptcy, consignors who were not the artists argued that the NY law protected their proceeds. Commentary noted that it covers artist-consignors only, and that a fiduciary duty does not by itself segregate funds from the estate. [JD Supra, Paddle 8 creditors battle over scope of NY consignment law](https://jdsupra.com/legalnews/paddle-8-creditors-battle-over-scope-of-38035)

## How it applies to Rescrow

- `declareInsolvency()` in `src/lib/escrow/engine.ts` moves the whole operating balance into one `claimedByCreditorsCents` number. Real bankruptcy splits the estate by class (§507, §726) with pro rata sharing inside a class. A class-by-class split would add real-world depth and could still reconcile to the cent.
- The legal strength of Rescrow is tracing, not labels. Because each hold is funded by a specific bank transaction and swept at once, the "connection" _Begier_ asks for and the running balance LIBR needs are already in the data. Commingling is what destroys a claim, and Rescrow never commingles.
- Payouts from trust are not preferences (_Begier_), while the same payout made from a mixed operating account is exposed (_Wendy's_). That gives the demo a second failure mode: artists paid by a commingling gallery can be asked to pay the money back.
- Today the insolvency beat behaves like chapter 7: the gallery is frozen (`assertActive` throws) and trust pays out at once. Chapter 11 is the more common business case. The gallery keeps trading, and new installments must still be split into trust after the filing date.
- The honest claim is narrow. NY's statute says trust funds are outside the estate, and the code enforces segregation so that a trustee faces no tracing fight. Rescrow should not claim a court has ruled on this, because none has in a gallery bankruptcy that I found.

## Feature ideas

### LIBR tracer: what the artist could recover without Rescrow

- What: a pure function `traceLIBR(events, claimantId)` replays the same transaction stream as if every dollar sat in one commingled account. It returns the running balance, the artist's claim, and the recoverable amount (the minimum balance since each deposit, capped at the claim, never refilled by later deposits). The insolvency screen draws two lines over time: the account balance and the artist's claim. The dip where the claim is lost is shaded. A golden test uses the _Hill v. Kinzler_ numbers (10k + 10k in, 15k out, 5k back in, so 5k recoverable).
- Why judges care: it shows the tracing rule a court would actually use, computed exactly from the ledger. This is different from the existing "without Rescrow" waterfall idea in `gallery-collapses.md`, which assumes the artist ranks unsecured with no tracing claim.
- Demo visibility: both
- Effort: M
- Demo risk: low. It is a read-only replay. The chart needs a scripted payroll or rent withdrawal so the dip is visible.
- Grounding: [Hill v. Kinzler](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/us-10th-circuit/1004530.html), [Patterson Belknap on tracing](https://www.pbwt.com/bankruptcy-update-blog/navigating-asset-tracing-challenges-in-bankruptcy)

### US priority waterfall inside `declareInsolvency`

- What: replace the single `claimedByCreditorsCents` with a `claims[]` register. Secured claims are paid from their collateral first, outside §726. Each other claim has a class (administrative, wages §507(a)(4) capped at $17,150 within 180 days, taxes §507(a)(8), general unsecured), and the operating estate pays out class by class. Inside a class that cannot be paid in full, claims share pro rata using largest-remainder rounding, so cents sum exactly. Trust holds appear in a separate "outside the estate, §541(d)" lane that the waterfall cannot read. `reconcile()` gains a check that the sum of distributions equals the claimed operating balance.
- Why judges care: it is real statutory ordering with exact integer rounding. It applies to the live demo gallery, unlike the existing UK-figures waterfall idea, and it plugs into the code path that already runs on screen.
- Demo visibility: both
- Effort: M
- Demo risk: low. The creditor amounts are seed data and should be labelled illustrative.
- Grounding: [§507](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/507), [§726](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/726), from the codebase (`declareInsolvency`, `reconcile`)

### Preference-exposure badge on every payout

- What: `preferenceExposure(snapshot, petitionAt)` classifies each payout. Payouts from trust holds are "not the debtor's property: not avoidable (_Begier_)". A payout drawn from operating, as in a commingled replay, gets a risk check: within 90 days of filing (one year if the payee is flagged as an insider), aggregate at least $8,575, and on an old debt. The payout list shows a green "safe" or a red "exposed" badge and shades the 90-day lookback window on the timeline. This builds on the existing "payout provenance" idea in `art-market-economics.md` by adding the §547 windows and thresholds.
- Why judges care: it covers the second way artists lose money, having to repay what they were already paid, with dates and dollar thresholds taken from the statute.
- Demo visibility: both
- Effort: S
- Demo risk: low. It is a pure read over existing payouts. The label must say "risk indicator", not legal advice.
- Grounding: [§547](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/547), [Begier v. IRS](https://chanrobles.com/usa/us_supremecourt/496/53/case.php), [IRS memo on commingled remittances](https://www.eitc.irs.gov/pub/irs-wd/0029002.pdf)

### Chapter 7 vs chapter 11 mode

- What: `declareInsolvency` takes `chapter: 7 | 11`. Chapter 7 is today's behaviour: operating is frozen for the trustee, and trust pays out at once. In chapter 11 the gallery keeps trading as debtor in possession. After filing, `receivePayment` and `releaseDueHolds` stay open, so holds keep their normal `dueBy` or completion payout and new installments still split into trust (see the next idea). Operating outflows are blocked by the automatic-stay guard below. A mode chip on the insolvency screen names the chapter. Later: a `convertToChapter7()` event.
- Why judges care: it shows the trust rule holds in both real failure modes, including the more common one where the business keeps running.
- Demo visibility: both
- Effort: M
- Demo risk: med. It changes the insolvency route the recorded demo uses, so `demo-flow.test.ts` must cover both paths.
- Grounding: [Chapter 11 basics](https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-11-bankruptcy-basics), [Chapter 7 basics](https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-7-bankruptcy-basics), from the codebase (`assertActive`)

### Post-filing receipts still split into trust

- What: today `receivePayment` calls `assertActive`, so it throws once a gallery is insolvent. A buyer's installment that lands after the filing date should still be ingested, idempotently on the transaction id. The artist's share goes straight to trust and pays out, and only the commission joins the estate. Invariant test: for any event order, receipts after the filing never increase operating by more than the commission.
- Why judges care: buyers keep paying after a failure, and this closes a real gap without letting the estate capture trust money.
- Demo visibility: repo-rigor
- Effort: S
- Demo risk: low. It is a backend rule with a property test and no UI change needed.
- Grounding: [NY statute via Legal 500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/united-kingdom/media-telecoms-it-entertainment/news-for-art-dealers-artists-and-artists%E2%80%99-estates-new-york%E2%80%99s-revised-artist-consignment-statute), from the codebase (`assertActive`, `receivePayment`)

### Automatic-stay guard on operating

- What: once a filing date is set, any operating outflow without a `courtOrderId` throws `EscrowError("automatic stay")`. Trust releases to the artist or buyer stay allowed, because that money is outside the estate. A table-driven test lists every money-moving function and checks it before and after the filing. This is the guard the chapter 11 mode relies on, so it can be built first, on its own.
- Why judges care: it is another "the rule is code" moment, now on the gallery's side of the ledger.
- Demo visibility: repo-rigor
- Effort: S
- Demo risk: low. It only adds a guard and tests.
- Grounding: [Chapter 7 basics (automatic stay)](https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-7-bankruptcy-basics), [Hill v. Kinzler (transfers in the gap after filing)](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/us-10th-circuit/1004530.html)

### Commingling clock on every hold

- What: each hold records `landedAt` (bank transaction time) and `sweptAt` (moved into trust). An invariant says the two are equal or within N seconds, and the max gap appears on the reconcile panel as "longest time any artist dollar sat in operating: 0 s". This is the "connection" evidence _Begier_ asks for, kept as data.
- Why judges care: commingling is the exact fact that loses trust claims in court, and this measures it as a single number.
- Demo visibility: both
- Effort: S
- Demo risk: low. It is two timestamps and a pure function.
- Grounding: [Begier v. IRS](https://chanrobles.com/usa/us_supremecourt/496/53/case.php), [Withers on mixed funds](https://www.withersworldwide.com/en-gb/insight/read/gallery-closures-–-protecting-artists)

### Trust-shortfall allocator ("equality is equity")

- What: `allocateShortfall(trustCents, claims[])` splits a pooled trust that cannot cover every artist pro rata, by largest remainder, and never favours whoever asked first. A property test, run on thousands of random event sequences, shows Rescrow can never reach this state, because trust balance always equals the sum of locked holds. The allocator exists only as a proven-unreachable fallback.
- Why judges care: it shows the team thought about the failure beyond the failure, using the Supreme Court's own rule for a short fund.
- Demo visibility: repo-rigor
- Effort: S
- Demo risk: low. It is not on screen.
- Grounding: [Cunningham v. Brown](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/265/1), [Hill v. Kinzler](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/us-10th-circuit/1004530.html)

### Lookback timeline scrubber

- What: one horizontal timeline for the insolvency screen. It shows the filing date, the shaded 90-day and one-year windows, each payment landing and splitting, and each payout as a dot coloured by preference status. Under it, the operating and trust balances are drawn as two lines. Scrubbing back shows the snapshot at that moment, using the existing event log.
- Why judges care: in one picture, the money and the legal windows line up on the same time axis.
- Demo visibility: shown-on-screen
- Effort: M
- Demo risk: med. It is new UI on the climax screen, and it must not slow polling.
- Grounding: [§547](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/547), from the codebase (event log, Snapshot)

### Debtor-in-possession monthly operating report

- What: `monthlyOperatingReport(snapshot, month)` builds the chapter 11 report from the ledger. It covers receipts, disbursements and the ending cash of the estate. Trust receipts and payouts sit in a separate schedule marked "held for others, not estate property". Every line comes from `src/lib`, and the report's totals must match `reconcile()`.
- Why judges care: it is the real filing a failing gallery owes the court, produced from the same books to the cent.
- Demo visibility: both
- Effort: M
- Demo risk: low. It is a printable page that reads existing data.
- Grounding: [Chapter 11 basics (Rule 2015 reports)](https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-11-bankruptcy-basics)

### Jurisdiction note on the insolvency card: statute vs declared trust

- What: the insolvency card names the legal basis for the gallery's jurisdiction. For NY it shows the statutory trust (§12.01, as amended in 2012). For England it shows a declared trust over a separate account (_Re Kayford_), plus a one-line warning that payments can be clawed back for up to two years. A test checks that every gallery has a basis set before its first sale. This extends the existing jurisdiction-profile idea with the insolvency-specific wording and the clawback window.
- Why judges care: it is honest that the protection differs by country, and the hook story is a UK case.
- Demo visibility: shown-on-screen
- Effort: S
- Demo risk: low. It is static copy chosen by a field.
- Grounding: [Re Kayford Ltd](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Re_Kayford), [Withers](https://www.withersworldwide.com/en-gb/insight/read/gallery-closures-–-protecting-artists), [Legal 500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/united-kingdom/media-telecoms-it-entertainment/news-for-art-dealers-artists-and-artists%E2%80%99-estates-new-york%E2%80%99s-revised-artist-consignment-statute)
