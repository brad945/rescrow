# Gallery collapses and dealer frauds: what failed, and what Rescrow would have stopped

Note: several of the cases below are still allegations, not court findings (The Hole, Kavi Gupta, Jack Shainman). They are marked that way. Knoedler was a forgery case. It is included here as a contrast, because a payments ledger does nothing about fakes.

## What we learned

- **Stephen Friedman Gallery (London, 2026):** administration filings show about £7.8m of debt. More than £800,000 of it is owed to artists: Alexandre Diop £341,905, Deborah Roberts £289,232, Kehinde Wiley £163,849. Unsecured creditors are expected to recover 8 to 9 pence per pound. HMRC (£550,000) and the Cork Street landlord (£505,113) are also on the list. [The Art Newspaper, 30 Apr 2026](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2026/04/30/stephen-friedman-gallery-owes-£800000-to-artists-filings-reveal); [Withers](https://www.withersworldwide.com/en-gb/insight/read/gallery-closures-–-protecting-artists)
- **In England the artist is usually just an unsecured creditor.** Withers says arguing that sale proceeds were held in trust is hard once the money has been mixed into the gallery's own accounts. A contract clause that promises a segregated account may only give the artist a breach-of-contract claim against a company that has nothing left. Specific, identifiable artworks are much easier to get back than mixed-in cash. [Withers](https://www.withersworldwide.com/en-gb/insight/read/gallery-closures-–-protecting-artists)
- **UK clawback risk:** payments received after a winding-up petition, or paid to connected parties in the 6 months to 2 years before insolvency, can be challenged as preferences. Storage firms the gallery owes can claim a lien and hold artworks the artist actually owns. [Withers](https://www.withersworldwide.com/en-gb/insight/read/gallery-closures-–-protecting-artists)
- **NY Arts and Cultural Affairs Law §12.01** makes consigned work "trust property" and sale proceeds "trust funds". They are not subject to the dealer's creditors or security interests. The trust-funds clause is the only part that can be waived, and the waiver can never cover the first $2,500 of proceeds in any 12-month period. If the dealer buys the work for itself, it stays trust property until the artist is paid in full. The statute sets no payment deadline. [NY Senate, ACA §12.01](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ACA/12.01)
- **Inigo Philbrick (2019 collapse, sentenced 2022):** sold more than 100% ownership of works to different buyers and used works as loan collateral without co-owners knowing. Named works include a Stingel, a Wool and Basquiat's _Humidity_. The scheme came to light after a $14m loan default. He got 7 years and $86m in restitution. [The Art Newspaper](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2022/05/24/inigo-philbrick-sentenced-to-seven-years-in-prison); [DOJ SDNY](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/pr/former-art-dealer-sentenced-7-years-86-million-fraud-scheme)
- **One Stingel shows the double-sale:** filings say Philbrick sold a 50% share to Satfinance for $3.35m in 2016, then sold the painting to Guzzini Properties in 2017. A third party says it agreed to buy it for $7.1m. Christie's sold it for $6.5m and then held it while the courts decided who owned it. [Artnet](https://news.artnet.com/art-world/mystery-surrounding-inigo-philbrick-deepens-claims-6m-painting-keep-piling-1732929); [Grossman LLP](https://www.grossmanllp.com/rudolf-stingel-painting-at-center-of-inigo-philbr)
- **Lawrence Salander (Salander-O'Reilly, closed 2007):** pleaded guilty in 2010 to 29 counts of grand larceny, about $120m, and was sentenced to 6 to 18 years. He admitted selling art he did not own and selling half-shares in the same work more than once. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Salander); [Center for Art Law](https://itsartlaw.org/art-law/new-yorks-southern-district-court-rules-that-artworks-consigned-to-galleries-may-be-reached-by-creditors/)
- **Salander and the De Niro Sr. estate:** works were sold without telling the estate, which lost about $1.25m. Reports say Salander signed some of the paintings over to a Venice gallery to pay his own debt. Robert De Niro later paid $14,000 to get back six paintings held by the bankruptcy court. [Village Voice](https://www.villagevoice.com/robert-de-niro-gets-some-of-dads-paintings-back-from-art-scamster-other-creditors-get-auction/); [Courthouse News](https://courthousenews.com/problems-mount-for-new-york-art-dealer/)
- **Consigned works can still end up with creditors:** in the Salander bankruptcy, a federal court in New York (2012) upheld a ruling that state law may let a bankrupt gallery sell a consigned work, here a Botticelli-attributed panel, for its creditors' benefit. [Center for Art Law](https://itsartlaw.org/art-law/new-yorks-southern-district-court-rules-that-artworks-consigned-to-galleries-may-be-reached-by-creditors/)
- **Berry-Hill Galleries (Chapter 11, Dec 2005):** the gallery had given lender ACG a security interest in all its art, valued at over $50m, against a $20.1m loan. Separately, a collector sued for a declaration that the 50% interest he had bought in Bellows' _Kids_ was free of those claims. A later suit by 624 Art Holdings alleges consigned paintings were sold or traded and the owner was never paid. [The Art Newspaper](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/archive/debt-and-legal-troubles-force-berry-hill-galleries-to-file-for-bankruptcy); [Courthouse News](https://www.courthousenews.com/?p=325186)
- **The Hole (New York, suit filed 2026, allegations):** two artists say the gallery's own books show it paid Daniel Um $20,207.50 of $49,182.50 and Nastaran Shahbazi $62,234.50 of $83,235.00. They say the money went to "rent, operating expenses, [and] other creditors" instead of being set aside, and that they were told business was slow. [amNY](https://www.amny.com/law/artists-sue-the-hole-bowery-theft-pay/); [Hyperallergic](https://hyperallergic.com/artists-sue-nyc-gallery-the-hole-for-nonpayment/)
- **Cadence Comic Art (SDNY, 2025, settled):** 17 artists alleged over $473,000 was never remitted. The complaint describes "secret sales and secret transfers", commingled trust funds, inaccurate statements, and more than 49 unsold pieces kept after a formal demand. [The Beat](https://www.comicsbeat.com/cadence-comic-art-lawsuit-has-been-settled)
- **Jeffrey Gibson v. Kavi Gupta (allegations on both sides):** the artist says $541,852 was withheld and only framing should come off before the 50/50 split. The gallery says production and promotion costs come off the top, and wants to offset $786,600 it says he owes it, including pandemic-era advances. [Hyperallergic](https://hyperallergic.com/artist-jeffrey-gibson-sues-chicago-gallery)
- **Odita v. Jack Shainman (2025, allegations):** a monthly stipend replaced the proceeds split. When sales slowed, the gallery kept selling works and applied the proceeds to a "deficit" it says is nearly $300,000. The artist says it is under $180,000. The gallery argues the deal was a secured loan under UCC Article 9, not a consignment. [Grossman LLP](https://www.grossmanllp.com/shainman-odita-artist-gallery-relationship)
- **Mary Boone (sentenced 2019):** more than $1.6m of gallery money paid for personal costs, including a roughly $900,000 home remodel. The gallery reported a loss for a year in which it made about $3.7m profit. She got 30 months. This was tax fraud, not artist theft, but it shows how loosely one owner can control a gallery's books. [Hyperallergic](https://hyperallergic.com/480605/mary-boone-sentenced-to-30-months-in-prison/)
- **Knoedler (closed 2011):** sold more than 30 forged Abstract Expressionist works, about $60m in total, from one source over 14 years. It closed after a buyer sued over a fake Pollock. This failure was about authenticity, not where the money went. [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/the-rise-and-fall-of-the-knoedler-new-yorks-most-notorious-art-gallery-53775); [Winterthur, Treasures on Trial](https://treasuresontrial.winterthur.org/knoedler/)

## How it applies to Rescrow

- **Commingling is the most common failure** (Stephen Friedman, The Hole, Cadence). Rescrow already answers it: the artist's share never touches operating, so there is nothing to trace afterwards. That is exactly the argument Withers says is hard to win in England once money has been mixed.
- **Double-selling and over-selling** (Philbrick, Salander, possibly Berry-Hill) are the second pattern, and Rescrow does not cover them yet. Today an `Artwork` has a single `status` and no ownership-share or lien model. A title-and-encumbrance ledger with a hard 100% cap would extend "the rule is code" from money to title.
- **The fights over deductions, offsets and stipends** (Gibson, Odita) are really about what may come out of the artist's share. Rescrow's single exit, `releaseTrust(to: artist|buyer)`, already rules out a gallery offset. Making that refusal visible, and giving deductions a cited clause and a cap, would turn a lawsuit-sized dispute into a rule.
- **Secret sales and inaccurate statements** (Cadence, Salander, Boone's books) call for a ledger the artist can check without trusting the gallery: a notice for every sale, and a tamper-evident activity log.
- **The insolvency beat can use real numbers.** Run the same events through a commingled ledger with an administrator's waterfall, and the artist gets pence on the pound. Through Rescrow, they get 100%.
- **Be honest about limits:** Rescrow does nothing about forgeries (Knoedler) or works held by a storage firm. Saying so in the demo builds credibility.

## Feature ideas

### Title ledger with a 100% ownership cap

- What: add `OwnershipShare { artworkId, holderId, bps }` and one function, `transferShare()`, that throws if the shares for a work would add up to more than 10000 bps, or if the work already has an open sale. `createSale` checks it first. A property test runs random sequences of share sales and asserts no work ever passes 100%.
- Why judges care: the same "refuse in code" rule as trust, applied to the Philbrick and Salander fraud.
- Demo visibility: both
- Effort: M
- Demo risk: low. It is a pure function, and the demo only shows a refused second sale with an error toast.
- Grounding: [The Art Newspaper](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2022/05/24/inigo-philbrick-sentenced-to-seven-years-in-prison), [Artnet](https://news.artnet.com/art-world/mystery-surrounding-inigo-philbrick-deepens-claims-6m-painting-keep-piling-1732929), [Center for Art Law](https://itsartlaw.org/art-law/new-yorks-southern-district-court-rules-that-artworks-consigned-to-galleries-may-be-reached-by-creditors/)

### Double-sell attempt button ("Sell it again")

- What: a demo key that tries to sell _Orchard in Rain_ to a second collector while the first sale is open. The engine throws `EscrowError("already under contract")`, and the activity feed logs a red "refused" row. It reuses the title ledger above.
- Why judges care: gives the Philbrick story a visible failure case, the way the insolvency beat does for Stephen Friedman.
- Demo visibility: shown-on-screen
- Effort: S, once the title ledger exists
- Demo risk: low. The answer is fixed, and nothing about the money changes.
- Grounding: [Grossman LLP on the Stingel](https://www.grossmanllp.com/rudolf-stingel-painting-at-center-of-inigo-philbr)

### Lien guard: consigned work cannot be collateral

- What: add `Encumbrance { artworkId, lenderName, createdAt }` and a `pledgeArtwork()` function that throws for any work whose status is `consigned`, citing §12.01's "not subject to... security interests". Only works the gallery owns outright can be pledged. A test covers both branches.
- Why judges care: Berry-Hill pledged "all its art" to a lender, and Philbrick borrowed against works he did not own outright.
- Demo visibility: repo-rigor
- Effort: S
- Demo risk: low. It is a test-only path.
- Grounding: [The Art Newspaper on Berry-Hill](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/archive/debt-and-legal-troubles-force-berry-hill-galleries-to-file-for-bankruptcy), [NY ACA §12.01](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ACA/12.01)

### "Without Rescrow" counterfactual waterfall

- What: a pure function `commingledOutcome(snapshot)` that replays the same events as if every dollar had landed in one account. It then runs a simple administrator's waterfall: secured lender first, then the tax authority, then unsecured creditors pro rata, in integer cents with a running-total remainder. The insolvency screen shows two bars side by side: "commingled: 8.5p per £1" and "Rescrow: 100p per £1".
- Why judges care: it turns the hook into a number computed live from the same ledger, not a claim.
- Demo visibility: both
- Effort: M
- Demo risk: med. The creditor amounts are seeded assumptions and must be labelled as illustrative so they don't read as Stephen Friedman's real figures.
- Grounding: [The Art Newspaper, Apr 2026](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2026/04/30/stephen-friedman-gallery-owes-£800000-to-artists-filings-reveal), [Withers](https://www.withersworldwide.com/en-gb/insight/read/gallery-closures-–-protecting-artists)

### Offset and deduction guard with cited clauses

- What: add `Deduction { saleId, kind, cents, clauseQuote }`. A deduction is allowed only if its kind (for example `framing`) appears in the agreement's extracted terms with a quote found word for word and a cap, and it comes off before the split. Any "offset against advances" or "deficit recovery" is refused, because trust can only go to the artist or the buyer. The statement lists each deduction with its quote.
- Why judges care: the Gibson and Odita disputes are about exactly this. Here it is a rule with a test instead of a lawsuit.
- Demo visibility: both
- Effort: M
- Demo risk: low. The saved agreement already mentions framing (the PRD's tier-2 Theo framing cap), so this is a fixed path.
- Grounding: [Hyperallergic on Gibson v. Kavi Gupta](https://hyperallergic.com/artist-jeffrey-gibson-sues-chicago-gallery), [Grossman LLP on Odita](https://www.grossmanllp.com/shainman-odita-artist-gallery-relationship), from the codebase (`releaseTrust`)

### Tamper-evident activity log (hash chain)

- What: each `Activity` gets `prevHash` and `hash = sha256(prevHash + canonical JSON)`, using Node's crypto with no network. `verifyLog(snapshot)` returns the first broken link. The artist portal shows a "ledger verified" badge, and a test edits an old row and expects the break to be found.
- Why judges care: Cadence's alleged inaccurate statements and Boone's falsified books both depend on the gallery being able to rewrite history.
- Demo visibility: both
- Effort: M
- Demo risk: low. It is deterministic. The risk is reset and seed paths forgetting to rebuild the chain, which a test can catch.
- Grounding: [The Beat on Cadence](https://www.comicsbeat.com/cadence-comic-art-lawsuit-has-been-settled), [Hyperallergic on Boone](https://hyperallergic.com/480605/mary-boone-sentenced-to-30-months-in-prison/)

### Sale notice to the artist, with no silent sales

- What: `createSale` and every `receivePayment` push an artist-facing notice into the artist portal feed (buyer, price, installment plan, the artist's share, dueBy). There is deliberately no code path to create a sale without that notice, and a test asserts each sale has exactly one notice.
- Why judges care: "secret sales" were central to the Cadence and Salander cases. Here the artist sees the sale the moment it happens.
- Demo visibility: shown-on-screen
- Effort: S
- Demo risk: low. The phone mock already shows artist events.
- Grounding: [The Beat](https://www.comicsbeat.com/cadence-comic-art-lawsuit-has-been-settled), [Village Voice via search](https://www.villagevoice.com/robert-de-niro-gets-some-of-dads-paintings-back-from-art-scamster-other-creditors-get-auction/)

### Per-artist reconciliation and statement to the cent

- What: extend `reconcile()` with `reconcileArtist(s, artistId)`: the artist's share of everything received equals their locked trust plus payouts plus refunds, to the cent. Each line links to the Ramp transaction id and the hold id. The statement shows "owed vs paid" like the figures in The Hole complaint, which here always differ by $0.00.
- Why judges care: the books balance for each artist, not just in total, which is what an artist or an administrator actually asks for.
- Demo visibility: both
- Effort: S
- Demo risk: low. It is a pure function over existing data.
- Grounding: [amNY on The Hole](https://www.amny.com/law/artists-sue-the-hole-bowery-theft-pay/), from the codebase (`reconcile.ts`)

### Gallery-as-buyer rule (§12.01 self-purchase)

- What: if the buyer on a sale is the gallery itself, the work keeps a `trustUntilPaid` flag. It can't be resold or pledged until the artist's share for that sale is fully paid, and a resale before then routes proceeds into trust up to the unpaid balance. This is a direct encoding of the statute's clause, with tests.
- Why judges care: it shows the engine models the actual law, edge cases included.
- Demo visibility: repo-rigor
- Effort: M
- Demo risk: low. It is not in the main demo path.
- Grounding: [NY ACA §12.01](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ACA/12.01)

### Waiver-clause detector with a $2,500 floor

- What: the saved agreement checks scan for waiver language with a fixed phrase list, using the same quote-snapping as the existing citations. If a waiver is found, the agent adds a `VerificationCheck` explaining that Rescrow ignores it, because trust still applies. It also shows the statutory floor (the first $2,500 of proceeds per 12 months can never be waived).
- Why judges care: the "agent shows its work" pillar applied to a real statutory trap, with no model needed.
- Demo visibility: both
- Effort: S
- Demo risk: low. It is deterministic string matching on a fixture agreement.
- Grounding: [NY ACA §12.01](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ACA/12.01), from the codebase (`VerificationCheck`)

### Consigned inventory return on insolvency

- What: on `declareInsolvency`, every artwork still `consigned` gets a `returnDue` row, shown as "Return to artist, not an asset of the estate". Optional fields record who holds it (gallery or a storage firm) so a possible storage lien can be flagged. The creditor-claim function excludes these works from anything it reports as an asset.
- Why judges care: it extends the insolvency beat from cash to the paintings themselves. Cadence kept 49 pieces, and Salander's estate sold a consigned Botticelli.
- Demo visibility: both
- Effort: S
- Demo risk: low. It adds rows to an existing beat.
- Grounding: [Withers](https://www.withersworldwide.com/en-gb/insight/read/gallery-closures-–-protecting-artists), [The Beat](https://www.comicsbeat.com/cadence-comic-art-lawsuit-has-been-settled), [Center for Art Law](https://itsartlaw.org/art-law/new-yorks-southern-district-court-rules-that-artworks-consigned-to-galleries-may-be-reached-by-creditors/)

### Administrator's packet export

- What: a printable page built only from `src/lib` functions. It lists each artist's trust paid in full (with hold and transaction ids and timestamps showing the money never sat in operating), the operating balance handed to creditors, and the reconciliation line. It explains why trust payouts made close to insolvency aren't preference payments: the money was never the gallery's.
- Why judges care: real fidelity. This is the document an insolvency practitioner would ask for.
- Demo visibility: shown-on-screen
- Effort: M
- Demo risk: med. It is print styling, and the clawback wording must stay factual rather than sound like legal advice.
- Grounding: [Withers](https://www.withersworldwide.com/en-gb/insight/read/gallery-closures-–-protecting-artists)

### Stipend and advance model kept outside trust

- What: add `Advance { artistId, cents, paidAt }`. Advances come only from operating and show on the artist's statement as a separate receivable. The engine refuses to net them against holds. An artist can repay only by an explicit, artist-initiated payment after a payout. A test confirms holds are untouched when an advance balance is outstanding.
- Why judges care: Odita's stipend "deficit" and Gupta's $786,600 offset claim show where artist-gallery money fights end up.
- Demo visibility: repo-rigor
- Effort: M
- Demo risk: low. It stays off the main demo path.
- Grounding: [Grossman LLP on Odita](https://www.grossmanllp.com/shainman-odita-artist-gallery-relationship), [Hyperallergic on Gibson](https://hyperallergic.com/artist-jeffrey-gibson-sues-chicago-gallery)

### Case-file overlay: "which rule would have stopped it"

- What: a static panel on `/present` that maps each real case (Stephen Friedman, The Hole, Cadence, Philbrick, Salander, Berry-Hill) to the Rescrow function and test that blocks it. Each row links to the test name. It openly lists Knoedler (forgery) as out of scope.
- Why judges care: in one screen it shows the design came from real failures, with honest limits.
- Demo visibility: shown-on-screen
- Effort: S
- Demo risk: low. It is static content, but must name cases carefully, marking allegations as allegations.
- Grounding: all sources above
