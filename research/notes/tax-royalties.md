# Tax and royalty obligations around art sales

Scope: US information returns (1099-NEC, 1099-K), backup withholding, sales and use tax on art, resale royalties (EU droit de suite, UK Artist's Resale Right, California Resale Royalty Act), and withholding for foreign artists. Nothing here is tax advice. Points marked "unverified" are modeling choices, not settled law.

## What we learned

- **1099-NEC threshold rose to $2,000 for payments made in 2026.** Payments in 2025 and earlier still use $600, and the threshold is indexed for inflation from 2027. The test applies per payee per calendar year, keyed on when the payment is made. [IRS, Instructions for Forms 1099-MISC and 1099-NEC](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099mec); [Patriot Software summary of OBBBA §70433](https://www.patriotsoftware.com/blog/accounting/1099-reporting-threshold/)
- **Payments for merchandise are excluded from 1099-NEC and 1099-MISC.** The IRS instructions list merchandise among payments that need no form. To a gallery, consigned artwork is merchandise, so consignment proceeds paid to an artist generally get no 1099. The same artist's fees for services, such as installation, framing labor or a talk, are reportable. [IRS instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099mec); [Hyperallergic, "Artists, Don't Make This Common Tax Mistake"](https://hyperallergic.com/artists-dont-make-this-common-tax-mistake)
- **Galleries often send these 1099s by mistake**, and artists must report all art income whether or not a form arrives. The Hyperallergic piece suggests artists keep two columns, "1099" and "non-1099", with each dollar in exactly one. [Hyperallergic](https://hyperallergic.com/artists-dont-make-this-common-tax-mistake)
- **Backup withholding is 24%.** It applies to reportable payments (1099 types such as contractor fees and royalties) when the payee has not given a correct TIN, usually collected on Form W-9. It never applies to merchandise payments, because those are not reportable. [IRS, Backup withholding](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/backup-withholding)
- **The 1099-K threshold reverted to over $20,000 and over 200 transactions**, and both tests must be met. The 1099-K is issued by payment settlement entities such as card processors and payment apps. A buyer's wire into the gallery's bank account doesn't make the gallery or Rescrow one. [IRS newsroom, 1099-K FAQs under OBBB](https://irs.gov/newsroom/irs-issues-faqs-on-form-1099-k-threshold-under-the-one-big-beautiful-bill-dollar-limit-reverts-to-20000)
- **New York treats consignment as an agency, not a sale.** A consignee that sells for a vendor collects and remits sales tax from buyers as the consignor's agent. [NY DTF advisory opinion TSB-A-81(27)S](https://tax.ny.gov/pdf/advisory_opinions/sales/h81_27s.pdf)
- **New York sales tax on art depends on where delivery happens.** Delivery in New York is taxable. Shipment by the seller, or by a common carrier, to a buyer out of state generally is not. A private or contract carrier hired by the buyer can make the sale taxable in New York, and dealers have paid large settlements over this distinction. [Frankfurt Kurnit, "Are art purchases by out-of-state residents subject to NY sales tax?"](https://fkks.com/news/are-art-purchases-by-out-of-state-residents-subject-to-new-york-sales-tax); [NY DTF advisory opinion TSB-A-16(17)S](https://www.tax.ny.gov/pdf/advisory_opinions/sales/a16_17s.pdf)
- **In New York, people who control a business's tax and accounting can be personally liable for unremitted sales tax**, whether or not it was collected. Practitioners describe collected sales tax as a trust fund tax. Whether that money is held in trust for the state is unverified here, so we treat it as a modeling choice. [The CPA Journal, personal liability for trust fund tax in New York](https://www.cpajournal.com/2026/04/14/the-scope-of-personal-liability-for-trust-fund-tax-in-the-state-of-new-york/)
- **The EU resale right (Directive 2001/84/EC) can't be waived and covers resales involving art market professionals.** That includes galleries and dealers acting as seller, buyer or intermediary. The seller pays, and member states may make the professional liable alone or jointly. Member states set the minimum price, at no more than €3,000. Rates are tiered from 4% down to 0.25%, member states may use 5% on the first band, and the total is capped at €12,500. Artists can demand sale information for three years. [Directive 2001/84/EC text (UCL mirror)](https://homepages.ucl.ac.uk/~ucqnmve/laws/rright.html)
- **UK ARR, from 1 April 2024, is in pounds.**
  - The threshold is £1,000, and the bands are cumulative like income tax: 4% to £50,000, 3% to £200,000, 1% to £350,000, 0.5% to £500,000 and 0.25% above. The cap is £12,500.
  - A £200,000 sale yields £6,500.
  - A "bought as stock" exception covers a work bought from the artist under three years earlier and resold for £10,000 or less.
  - DACS and ACS collect and distribute the royalty, and artists can't claim it directly from dealers.
  - [GOV.UK, Artist's Resale Right guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/artists-resale-right)
- **UK ARR at 20 years (2006 to 2026):** DACS has collected over £176m and distributed over £144m to 6,997 artists and heirs. The cap hasn't moved in 20 years. The US still has no resale right. [The Art Newspaper, Feb 2026](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2026/02/17/uk-artist-resale-rights-at-20-how-successful-has-pioneering-scheme-been)
- **California Resale Royalty Act (Civil Code §986), as written:**
  - It sets a 5% royalty on resales over $1,000 by a California seller or in California, with none owed if the price is below what the seller paid.
  - Galleries, dealers and auction houses acting for the seller must withhold the 5% and find the artist.
  - Money not paid within 90 days goes to the Arts Council. After 7 years unclaimed, the artist's right ends.
  - [California Civil Code §986 (public.law)](https://california.public.law/codes/civil_code_section_986)
- **CRRA is mostly dead law.** The Ninth Circuit's 2015 en banc ruling cut out the out-of-state-sales part on Commerce Clause grounds. Then _Close v. Sotheby's_ (9th Cir., July 2018) held the 1976 Copyright Act preempts claims for sales after 1 January 1978, leaving essentially 1977 sales. [Center for Art Law, case review](https://itsartlaw.org/case-review/case-review-droit-de-suite-not-so-sweet/)
- **Foreign artists:**
  - US payers withhold 30% by default on payments for independent services performed in the US by nonresident artists, and report on Forms 1042 and 1042-S. Anyone who controls or pays the income can be the withholding agent. [IRS, FAQs on foreign artist and athlete withholding](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/frequently-asked-questions-faqs-about-foreign-artist-and-athlete-withholding)
  - US-source royalties, such as a reproduction license, take 30% or a lower treaty rate claimed on Form W-8BEN. [IRS, Withholding on specific income](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/withholding-on-specific-income)
  - We found no IRS authority on a nonresident artist selling their own physical work through a US gallery. Treat that case as unresolved.

## How it applies to Rescrow

- **Rescrow tracks consignment proceeds, which are merchandise.** So the default answer for an artist payout is "no 1099-NEC." Showing that decision, and the reason for it, is better than issuing a wrong form. The only reportable lines are service fees the gallery pays the artist, such as installation or framing labor, and those don't go through trust.
- **Rescrow's ledger has no sales tax yet.** If a buyer pays price plus tax, the tax is neither the gallery's nor the artist's. It needs its own bucket, it must stay out of the artist split, and it must be refunded on cancellation. Otherwise `reconcile()` would quietly count tax as gallery revenue.
- **Rescrow's guarantee is that trust money leaves only through `releaseTrust(to: artist|buyer)`.** Every new obligation (state tax, a resale royalty, a custodian for an unreachable artist) must either fit through that function or get its own separately guarded ledger. It must never get a new exit from artist trust.
- **Thresholds and rates change on fixed dates**: $600 to $2,000 on 1 Jan 2026, euros to pounds on 1 Apr 2024, CRRA cut back by the 2018 ruling. An effective-dated rules table fits the existing deterministic, integer-cents design and gives clear boundary tests.
- **The agreement reader already extracts split and payoutDays from saved answers.** Clauses like "artist is responsible for own taxes", "resale royalty waived" or "delivery terms" can be added to the same saved-answer fixtures. A clause that tries to waive the EU or UK resale right is a nice validation failure, because the right can't be waived.

## Feature ideas

### Sales-tax ledger, separate from artist trust

- What: A sale gets `taxRateBps` and `deliveryMode`. Each payment splits three ways: tax, artist share and gallery share. The artist and gallery shares are computed on the pre-tax price, and tax gets the same running-total rounding as the artist share, so installments sum exactly. Tax goes to a `taxHolds` ledger whose only exit is `remitTax(to: "state")`, a guard separate from `releaseTrust`. `reconcile()` becomes `opening + received = operating + trust + paid + refunded + claimed + taxHeld + taxRemitted`.
- Why judges care: It shows the trust model generalizes to a second kind of money that isn't the gallery's, without loosening the first guard.
- Demo visibility: both
- Effort: L (touches createSale, receivePayment, cancelSale, declareInsolvency, reconcile and the split UI; M if backend only)
- Demo risk: med. It changes the core split path, but the existing 300-sale property test catches regressions.
- Grounding: [NY TSB-A-81(27)S](https://tax.ny.gov/pdf/advisory_opinions/sales/h81_27s.pdf); [CPA Journal on trust fund tax](https://www.cpajournal.com/2026/04/14/the-scope-of-personal-liability-for-trust-fund-tax-in-the-state-of-new-york/); from the codebase (`reconcile.ts`, `artistShareOfPayment`)

### Three-way insolvency split

- What: Built on the tax ledger. `declareInsolvency` sends artist trust to artists via `releaseTrust`, held tax to the state via `remitTax`, and only operating to creditors. The insolvency screen shows three columns that each sum to the cent. Remitting tax ahead of creditors is a modeling choice, labeled as such.
- Why judges care: It extends the demo climax: creditors get only what the gallery actually owned.
- Demo visibility: shown-on-screen
- Effort: M (after the tax ledger exists)
- Demo risk: med. It depends on the tax ledger shipping first, and the beat 6 script needs a new line.
- Grounding: [CPA Journal](https://www.cpajournal.com/2026/04/14/the-scope-of-personal-liability-for-trust-fund-tax-in-the-state-of-new-york/); from the codebase (`declareInsolvency`)

### Cancellation unwinds tax too

- What: `cancelSale` refunds the buyer the artist share from trust (as today), the gallery share from operating, and the tax portion from `taxHolds`. Tax already remitted becomes a `taxRefundClaim` receivable, never a negative balance. A test asserts the buyer gets back exactly what they paid, to the cent.
- Why judges care: Refund paths are where real ledgers break, and this one is proven.
- Demo visibility: repo-rigor
- Effort: M
- Demo risk: low. It's backend plus one line on the cancel screen.
- Grounding: [NY DTF advisory opinion TSB-A-20(41)S](https://www.tax.ny.gov/pdf/advisory_opinions/sales/a20-41s.pdf); from the codebase (`cancelSale`)

### Delivery-based taxability rule

- What: A pure `taxabilityFor(sale)` returns `{taxable, reason, citation}` from a small table. In-state pickup or delivery is taxable. Seller-arranged or common-carrier shipment out of state is not. A private carrier hired by the buyer and handed the work in New York is taxable. The sale card shows the reason line, for example "Shipped by common carrier to CT: no NY tax."
- Why judges care: It encodes a real rule that has cost dealers millions, deterministically and with a stated reason.
- Demo visibility: both
- Effort: S (rule plus tests); M with UI
- Demo risk: low. It's a pure function with a table-driven test.
- Grounding: [Frankfurt Kurnit](https://fkks.com/news/are-art-purchases-by-out-of-state-residents-subject-to-new-york-sales-tax); [TSB-A-16(17)S](https://www.tax.ny.gov/pdf/advisory_opinions/sales/a16_17s.pdf)

### Artist's Resale Right calculator with golden vectors

- What: A pure `resaleRoyalty(priceMinor, regime, saleDate)` uses integer pence or cents, cumulative bands, the threshold, the cap and the bought-as-stock exception. Rounding rule: compute each band in exact integer arithmetic, then round half up once on the total.
  - Golden tests: £200,000 → £6,500 (GOV.UK), £210,000 → £6,600 (DACS), £999.99 → 0, a sale big enough to hit the £12,500 cap, and an EU variant with a 5% first band.
  - Property tests: the royalty never decreases as price rises, never exceeds the cap, and has no jump at band edges.
- Why judges care: Tiered math with caps is where off-by-one and float bugs live, and this one is proven.
- Demo visibility: repo-rigor
- Effort: S
- Demo risk: low. It's standalone and touches no existing flow.
- Grounding: [GOV.UK ARR](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/artists-resale-right); [Directive 2001/84/EC Art. 3–4](https://homepages.ucl.ac.uk/~ucqnmve/laws/rright.html)

### Secondary-market sale with a royalty hold for the original artist

- What: A new sale type, `resale`, where the consignor is a collector, not the artist. §12.01 protects artist-consignors, so the collector's proceeds aren't artist trust. Rescrow computes the ARR royalty and locks only that slice in a hold whose beneficiary is the original artist, or the collecting society acting for them. It pays out through `releaseTrust(to: artist)` with a `via: "DACS"` tag.
- Why judges care: It shows the model knows whose money is whose, even when three parties are involved.
- Demo visibility: both
- Effort: L
- Demo risk: med. It adds a new party type and a new sale flow, and the beneficiary model needs care.
- Grounding: [GOV.UK ARR](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/artists-resale-right); [Directive Art. 1(4)](https://homepages.ucl.ac.uk/~ucqnmve/laws/rright.html); from the codebase (holds, `releaseTrust`)

### Effective-dated rules registry

- What: One `rules.ts` table of `{id, jurisdiction, validFrom, validTo, params, source}`. It covers 1099-NEC $600 (to 2025-12-31) and $2,000 (from 2026-01-01), UK ARR in euros (to 2024-03-31) and pounds (from 2024-04-01), and CRRA (1977 only after _Close v. Sotheby's_). The engine looks up the rule for an event's date and stamps the rule id on the ledger entry. A "rules" panel lists what applied to each line.
- Why judges care: Rules change on dates, and the ledger records which version applied, so old entries can be audited.
- Demo visibility: both
- Effort: M
- Demo risk: low. It's additive, with boundary-date tests.
- Grounding: [IRS 1099 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099mec); [GOV.UK ARR](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/artists-resale-right); [Center for Art Law](https://itsartlaw.org/case-review/case-review-droit-de-suite-not-so-sweet/)

### Information-return classifier (why no 1099)

- What: Each outflow to an artist gets `incomeType`, either `consignment_proceeds` (merchandise, not reportable) or `service_fee` (reportable). At year end, `informationReturns(year)` sums reportable payments per artist by payout date and flags those at or above that year's threshold.
  - The artist portal shows a two-column statement, "reportable / not reportable", where each payout sits in one column and the columns add up to total paid.
  - Boundary test: a hold received in December 2026 and paid in January 2027 counts in 2027.
- Why judges care: It gets a commonly botched rule right and explains it on screen.
- Demo visibility: both
- Effort: M
- Demo risk: low. It's read-only over existing payouts.
- Grounding: [IRS 1099 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099mec); [Hyperallergic](https://hyperallergic.com/artists-dont-make-this-common-tax-mistake)

### TIN gate with backup withholding on service lines only

- What: An artist record gets `tinOnFile: boolean`. A reportable service-fee payout to an artist without a TIN withholds 24% into a `withheldForPayee` sub-ledger, credited to the artist and counted in `paidToArtists`, so reconcile is unchanged. Consignment proceeds from trust are never withheld, and a test asserts it.
- Why judges care: It shows the system knows which payments a rule touches and which it doesn't.
- Demo visibility: repo-rigor
- Effort: M
- Demo risk: low. It sits outside the trust path.
- Grounding: [IRS backup withholding](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/backup-withholding); [IRS 1099 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099mec)

### Foreign-artist classifier that knows when to stop

- What: An artist record gets `residency` and `w8benOnFile`. A pure classifier maps `(residency, incomeType)` to an outcome:
  - US-performed service: 30%, Form 1042-S.
  - Reproduction-license royalty: 30%, or the treaty rate if a W-8BEN is on file.
  - Sale proceeds from a physical work: `needs_review`, with no rate.
  - Any `needs_review` blocks automatic payout until a person confirms, the same pattern as an unverified agreement quote.
- Why judges care: Saying "unknown" is more trustworthy than guessing a tax rate.
- Demo visibility: both
- Effort: M
- Demo risk: low. It's a pure classifier with a single gate.
- Grounding: [IRS foreign artist FAQ](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/frequently-asked-questions-faqs-about-foreign-artist-and-athlete-withholding); [IRS withholding on specific income](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/withholding-on-specific-income)

### Unreachable-artist custodian path

- What: Modeled on CRRA's 90-day and 7-year rules. If a payout fails because the artist's bank details are missing, the hold stays locked. After a configurable period, `releaseTrust(to: artist, via: custodian)` moves it to a custodian account labeled as held for the artist, still counted in paidToArtists. The gallery can never reclaim it. We chose this over adding a new exit from trust.
- Why judges care: It covers an edge case real payout systems hit, without weakening the one-exit rule.
- Demo visibility: repo-rigor
- Effort: M
- Demo risk: med. The `via` field touches `releaseTrust`'s signature and its trust tests.
- Grounding: [California Civil Code §986](https://california.public.law/codes/civil_code_section_986); from the codebase (`releaseTrust`)

### Clause checks for waivers that can't be waived

- What: Add saved-answer fixtures for agreement clauses such as "Artist waives any resale royalty" and "Artist is responsible for all taxes". A deterministic validator flags a resale-royalty waiver as unenforceable for EU or UK works, because the right can't be waived. It flags a California 5% waiver unless the clause gives more than 5%. Flags show next to the highlighted quote.
- Why judges care: The agreement reader stops just extracting numbers and starts checking them against law.
- Demo visibility: shown-on-screen
- Effort: S
- Demo risk: low. It uses saved answers and a fixed rule table.
- Grounding: [Directive Art. 1(1)](https://homepages.ucl.ac.uk/~ucqnmve/laws/rright.html); [California Civil Code §986](https://california.public.law/codes/civil_code_section_986)

### Year-end artist tax statement

- What: A printable per-artist, per-year statement lists each sale: gross price, commission, artist share, tax collected (shown, not the artist's), payout dates, and the reportable vs not-reportable split. Its totals are checked against `artistTotals` and `reconcile()`, and the statement refuses to render if they disagree.
- Why judges care: It's a document an artist's accountant would actually use, and it ties to the books to the cent.
- Demo visibility: shown-on-screen
- Effort: M
- Demo risk: low. It's read-only, and a printable statement is already in tier 2.
- Grounding: [Hyperallergic](https://hyperallergic.com/artists-dont-make-this-common-tax-mistake); from the codebase (`artistTotals`, `reconcile`)
